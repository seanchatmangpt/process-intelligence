# POST_003 — Activity is not evidence

## Draft Text (LinkedIn Post)

There is a distinction that almost no software team makes — and it is the distinction that governs
every audit, every compliance claim, every governance assertion about a running system.

**Activity is not evidence.**

An activity record says something happened. A timestamp says when. An actor ID says who. That is all
it says. It does not say whether what happened was permitted. It does not say whether it conformed
to what was declared. It does not say whether the boundary conditions were satisfied. It does not say
whether the closure was lawful.

Process evidence is different. Process evidence says what happened under a named witness, with typed
identity, admissible structure, refusal capability, and a replayable receipt. The evidence was either
admitted — accepted under a named authority — or refused, with the violated law named in the refusal.

The difference is not philosophical. It is structural.

A system that records activity can report what happened. A system that manufactures process evidence
can prove that a lawful process happened — or prove that it did not.

The second capability is the one that makes claims board-admissible. The second capability is what
full-lifecycle process intelligence provides. The first capability is what most software teams have.

Logs remember motion. Receipts prove consequence. No receipt, no closure.

The upstream dam must be built before the downstream data flows. By the time logs, dashboards, and
analytics appear, the governing act already happened.

Whoever controls admissible process truth controls downstream data, audit, governance, automation,
intelligence. That is the central law of process intelligence.

**Process Intelligence Public Canon** — weekly series grounding public claims in upstream ALIVE
receipts. No claim without a receipt. No receipt without a proof gate.
