## The Exclusion Law

Precision requires exclusion. A definition that does not name what it excludes is a description. The following exclusions are not hedges — they are hard boundaries enforced by the type law of this research program.

Claiming that process intelligence "includes" or "extends" the following categories is a category error. These categories are proper subsets, projections, or inputs — not equivalents.

---

## NOT: Process Mining

**What process mining is:** A family of algorithms for discovering, conforming, and enhancing process models from event logs. PM4Py is the reference implementation. Van der Aalst's canon is the research authority.

**Why process intelligence is not process mining:**
Process mining operates on completed event logs. It discovers what happened. Process intelligence manufactures what must happen, governs it through every lifecycle stage, receipts every transition, refuses unlawful inputs, and retires processes with archive authority.

Process mining is one subset of process intelligence — specifically, it occupies the Discovery, Conformance, and part of the Monitoring stages in the lifecycle calculus. It has no authority over Design, Simulation, Construction, Activation, Repair, BoardProjection, Integration, Decommission, or Archive.

**Formal statement:** PM4Py ∈ Omega (process intelligence algorithm space), but PM4Py ≠ process intelligence.

---

## NOT: Observability

**What observability is:** The property of a system that allows its internal state to be inferred from its external outputs. OTel (OpenTelemetry), distributed tracing, and metrics pipelines are observability infrastructure.

**Why process intelligence is not observability:**
Observability is one signal surface — it produces raw event evidence (OTel traces, spans, metrics) that can be converted into event logs. Process intelligence consumes observability signals as feedstock and manufactures lawful process reality from them.

Observability has no lifecycle authority. It cannot admit or refuse a process claim. It cannot emit a typed receipt. It cannot project a board-admissible slide. It cannot decommission a process with archive authority.

**Formal statement:** OTel/Weaver ∈ S (public standards feedstock), but OTel ≠ process intelligence.

---

## NOT: Telemetry

**What telemetry is:** The automated collection and transmission of measurements from remote sources. Telemetry is a projection — it reports what instruments observe.

**Why process intelligence is not telemetry:**
Telemetry is one projection channel. It can carry event evidence into a process intelligence system, but it has no manufacturing authority. Telemetry cannot actuate a transition, emit a receipt, or refuse an unlawful claim.

A dashboard that displays telemetry is not process intelligence. A system that collects telemetry is not process intelligence. The manufacturing that converts telemetry into receipted process evidence is process intelligence.

---

## NOT: AI Summarization

**What AI summarization is:** The application of language models to produce natural-language summaries of documents, logs, or data.

**Why process intelligence is not AI summarization:**
AI summarization has no lifecycle authority. It cannot emit typed receipts. It cannot refuse unlawful inputs with named laws. It cannot replay an execution. It cannot project a board-admissible claim that survives audit.

AI summarization produces descriptions. Process intelligence manufactures facts. A description of a process is not evidence of a process. Evidence requires receipt-bearing manufacture, type law enforcement, and replay capability.

**The key test:** Can you replay the summarization and produce the same receipted result? If not, it is summarization, not process intelligence.

---

## NOT: Dashboard Interpretation

**What dashboard interpretation is:** The human or automated reading of visual representations of data to produce narrative explanations.

**Why process intelligence is not dashboard interpretation:**
A dashboard has no receipt. A dashboard cannot be replayed. A dashboard cannot refuse an unlawful claim. A dashboard cannot decommission a process. A dashboard is a visualization — it is one possible output of a board projection, not the projection itself.

The distinction matters in due diligence: "Our dashboard shows X" is a claim without backing. "Our process intelligence manufactured X with receipt R, which can be replayed against event log L" is a board-admissible claim.

**Formal statement:** Dashboard ⊂ BoardProjection outputs, but Dashboard ≠ BoardProjection, and BoardProjection ≠ process intelligence.

---

## NOT: Software Development

**What software development is:** The engineering discipline of designing, constructing, and maintaining software systems.

**Why process intelligence is not software development:**
Software is one manufactured artifact within a process intelligence lifecycle. The `wasm4pm-compat` crate, the `wasm4pm` execution engine, and `ggen` are all software — they are manufacturing surfaces for process intelligence, not process intelligence itself.

Process intelligence governs the full lifecycle of business processes, including processes that produce no software. A supply chain process, a regulatory compliance process, or a capital allocation process are all process intelligence subjects. None of them are primarily software development.

**Formal statement:** Software ∈ Artifacts(process intelligence), but Software ≠ process intelligence.

## NOT: Consulting

**What consulting is:** The provision of expert advice, analysis, and recommendations to organizations on business problems.

**Why process intelligence is not consulting:**
Consulting describes. Process intelligence manufactures lawful reality.

A consulting report may describe what a process should look like. A process intelligence system manufactures the evidence that the process was lawfully designed, executed, monitored, repaired, and retired — with receipts at every stage that survive audit.

The Challenger Question makes this concrete:

> "If you claim to use public standards, can you manufacture an M&A-ready PowerPoint where every process claim is validated, traceable, replayable, and tied to public standards?"

A consulting firm answers: "Yes, we can write that PowerPoint."
A process intelligence system answers: "Yes, and here are the receipts, replay logs, and ALIVE gate attestations for every claim in it."

The difference is not sophistication — it is manufacturing authority versus descriptive authority.

## The Positive Definition

What process intelligence IS:

> Full-lifecycle manufacturing of lawful process reality from public standards, type law, receipts, replay, refusal, residuals, and lifecycle authority.

> A process is real when its design, execution, evidence, failure boundaries, repairs, projections, and retirement can be lawfully constructed, receipted, replayed, audited, and relied upon.

Everything in the exclusion list above is either a subset, a projection, a signal surface, or an input to this definition — not a substitute for it.
