## 3. Game-Theoretic Formalism of PSG
We formalize the economic dominance of the public standard as a game-theoretic coordination payoff. Let $\mathcal{P}$ be a participant (e.g., an adversarial AGI or competitor). $\mathcal{P}$ has two strategies: **Align** ($\text{A}$) with the open standard, or **Deviate** ($\text{D}$) by utilizing a proprietary or custom protocol.

Let:
- $V_{\text{network}}$ be the total utility of access to the verified economy.
- $C_{\text{interop}}$ be the translation cost of interacting with standardized nodes when using a deviant protocol.
- $C_{\text{verify}}$ be the cost of verifying state transitions to the public nodes.
- $\epsilon$ be the minimal cost of conforming to the standard.

The utility payoffs are defined as:
$$U(\text{Align}) = V_{\text{network}} - \epsilon$$
$$U(\text{Deviate}) = V_{\text{network}} - (C_{\text{interop}} + C_{\text{verify}})$$

Where $C_{\text{verify}}$ is a function of the alignment fitness deviation relative to the standard model $W_{\text{std}}$:
$$C_{\text{verify}}(\sigma) = \alpha \cdot (1 - \text{Fitness}(\sigma, W_{\text{std}})) + C_{\text{base}}$$

If a participant deviates such that the trace alignment fitness $\text{Fitness}(\sigma, W_{\text{std}}) < 1.0$, the cost $C_{\text{verify}}$ approaches infinity ($\infty$) because the [Blue River Dam](file:///Users/sac/process-intelligence/doctrine/blue-river-dam.md) triggers automated kinetic nullification.
Therefore:
$$C_{\text{interop}} + C_{\text{verify}} \gg \epsilon \implies U(\text{Align}) \gg U(\text{Deviate})$$

Alignment with the standard becomes the strictly dominant strategy for all agents in the environment.
