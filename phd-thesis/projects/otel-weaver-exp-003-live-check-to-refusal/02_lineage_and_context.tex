\section{Lineage and Context}
\label{sec:otel-weaver-exp-003-live-check-to-refusal-lineage}

\subsection{2016 Language-Model Lesson}
\label{sec:otel-weaver-exp-003-live-check-to-refusal-lineage-lm}

The thesis lineage originates in a 2016 language-model experiment that established a
foundational negative result: local text imitation does not conserve consequence.
A model trained to reproduce surface-level patterns of enterprise documents could emit
text that was structurally indistinguishable from authoritative records --- meeting
all schema and formatting criteria --- while carrying no causal or operational validity.
The model had learned the shape of consequence without the substance.

EXP-003 is, at its root, a mechanized restatement of this lesson applied to telemetry.
An OTel span that passes Weaver schema validation is the telemetry analog of the 2016
model's output: it has the correct shape. The attribute keys are present
(\texttt{process.pi.instance\_id}, \texttt{process.pi.activity.name},
\texttt{process.pi.witness.id}, \texttt{process.pi.witness.hash}), the types are
correct, the payload is non-null. But shape does not conserve consequence. A span
can be structurally perfect and causally fraudulent. The \texttt{validate\_and\_project}
gate addresses this by requiring not merely the presence of fields but the presence
of a witness signature that binds the span to a named law and a verifiable hash.
Without the 64-character BLAKE3 witness hash, the gate issues \texttt{RefusalCode::InvalidWitnessSignature}
regardless of how well-formed the rest of the feedstock appears.

\subsection{Enterprise Process Gap}
\label{sec:otel-weaver-exp-003-live-check-to-refusal-lineage-gap}

The 2016 lesson exposed a structural gap in enterprise process architectures: systems
that record process execution as telemetry and systems that validate process compliance
are almost universally decoupled. Observability tools confirm that instrumentation
is healthy; compliance tools confirm that processes follow normative models. The
boundary between the two --- the ingestion frontier where raw telemetry becomes
process evidence --- is rarely formalized.

In practice, this gap manifests as a trust assumption: telemetry that arrives at
the process-mining engine is assumed to have been lawfully emitted. This assumption
is neither verified at ingestion nor cryptographically sealed. When adversarial or
erroneous telemetry enters the conformance court through this gap, it produces false
conformance scores, hidden process variants, or silent compliance failures. The
\texttt{weaver-finding-is-not-receipt.md} doctrine document illustrates this attack
surface: ``an adversary could generate structurally perfect spans \ldots\ and inject
them into the telemetry stream to simulate completed transactions. A validation checker
running OTel Weaver would report a finding of 100\% compliance because the schema is
satisfied.'' EXP-003 closes this gap by making the frontier explicit and enforced.

\subsection{Relationship to the Chatman Equation}
\label{sec:otel-weaver-exp-003-live-check-to-refusal-lineage-equation}

The Chatman Equation $A = \mu(O^*)$ asserts that the authoritative state of an
enterprise system is the measure of its object model under its own operational laws.
Telemetry is an observation of $O^*$ --- a projection of the object model's runtime
behavior onto a stream of timestamped spans. The equation implies that $A$ cannot
be computed from telemetry alone, because the measure $\mu$ requires lawful object
lifecycle histories, not raw observations.

EXP-003 enforces this implication structurally. The \texttt{Admission<T, W>} type
represents feedstock that has been projected into a form that is lawful enough to
enter the measure computation: it carries a witness $W$ (the \texttt{AlphaWitness}
struct with a verified BLAKE3 hash) that names the law under which the observation
was made. A \texttt{Refusal} represents feedstock that failed to meet the minimum
conditions for projection --- it cannot participate in the measure computation because
it cannot be typed as \texttt{Admission}. The connection between EXP-003 and the
Chatman Equation is at the documentation level as of this writing; no formal type
binding from \texttt{Admission<T,W>} to the measure operator $\mu$ exists in the
current codebase. \textbf{(AUTHOR THESIS / INTERPRETATION)}

\subsection{Relationship to Receipts and Replay}
\label{sec:otel-weaver-exp-003-live-check-to-refusal-lineage-receipts}

The receipts-and-replay layer of the thesis lineage holds that every process state
transition must be grounded in a cryptographically sealed receipt that survives audit.
EXP-003 operates upstream of this layer: it determines what feedstock is admissible
as raw material for receipt manufacture. The \texttt{Admission<AdmittedPayload, AlphaWitness>}
value is not itself a receipt; it is the verified feedstock from which a receipt can
be manufactured by the \texttt{wasm4pm} conformance court. The \texttt{Refusal} is,
by contrast, a terminal artifact: it is a BLAKE3-digested record of what was rejected
and why, and it is the upstream equivalent of a receipt for non-conforming events.

The doctrine file \texttt{weaver-finding-is-not-receipt.md} formalizes this distinction
through a comparison table: a Weaver finding is ephemeral, software-generated diagnostic
output; a process receipt is ledger-bound, cryptographically signed, and permanently
part of the evidence chain. EXP-003 sits at the boundary between these two categories.
It takes ephemeral Weaver-style findings --- the four structural checks on
\texttt{instance\_id}, \texttt{activity\_name}, \texttt{witness\_id}, and
\texttt{witness\_hash} --- and either promotes them into the receipt-manufacturing
pipeline via \texttt{Admission} or archives them as structured refusal records. The
persistence layer for refusal records (a \texttt{refusals.json} ledger file described
in the README) is specified but not yet implemented; this is a documented gap.
