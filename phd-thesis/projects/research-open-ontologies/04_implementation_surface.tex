\section{Implementation Surface}
\label{sec:research-open-ontologies-implementation}

\subsection{Source Files}
\label{sec:research-open-ontologies-source-files}

The implementation surface spans two categories: ontology definition files (TTL) and the
manufacturing pipeline configuration (TOML, SPARQL, Tera).

\paragraph{Core Ontology Files (TTL).}
All 14 TTL files in \path{research/pi-program/ggen/ontology/} were validated in the Phase~7
roundtrip report as loading cleanly via rdflib~2.0.9 with no parse errors:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \texttt{checkpoint-ledger.ttl} --- 221 triples, 46 subjects. Records sealed
        checkpoints with their verdicts and timestamps.
  \item \texttt{conformance-ledger.ttl} --- 156 triples. Tracks conformance verdict
        instances with fitness and precision scores.
  \item \texttt{forbidden-collapse-law.ttl} --- 202 triples. Encodes the
        \texttt{pi:FORBIDDEN\_COLLAPSE} DTO boundary law.
  \item \texttt{graduation-boundary.ttl} --- 155 triples. Defines the boundary crossing
        conditions for artifact graduation from PARTIAL to ALIVE.
  \item \texttt{pi-ggen-audit-law.ttl} --- 327 triples, 71 subjects. Audit rule
        definitions for the ggen manufacturing conformance gates.
  \item \texttt{pi-ggen-checkpoint-ledger.ttl} --- 466 triples, 97 subjects, 91
        predicates. The largest single file; records the full checkpoint history of the
        ggen manufacturing ecosystem.
  \item \texttt{pi-ggen-generation-ledger.ttl} --- 316 triples. Records each
        manufactured artifact with its SPARQL rule provenance.
  \item \texttt{pi-ggen-invalid-extension-ledger.ttl} --- 260 triples. Records
        extension violations detected during ggen audit cycles.
  \item \texttt{pi-ggen-project-registry.ttl} --- 349 triples, 40 predicates.
        Registers all projects with their roles under \texttt{pi:ProgramRole}.
  \item \texttt{pi-ggen-source-ledger.ttl} --- 513 triples, 66 subjects. The
        largest source ledger; records all research artifacts with their provenance.
  \item \texttt{pi-ggen-unified-run.ttl} --- 168 triples. Records the Phase~11
        unified ggen run with gate outcomes.
  \item \texttt{pi-program.ttl} --- 176 triples. Top-level program ontology
        importing PROV-O, SKOS, DCTERMS, DCAT, SHACL, OWL.
  \item \texttt{project-registry.ttl} --- 195 triples. Cross-project dependency
        registry at the program level.
  \item \texttt{research-artifact-ledger.ttl} --- 238 triples. Comprehensive ledger
        of 342 research artifacts classified by type and phase.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Commands}
\label{sec:research-open-ontologies-commands}

The primary audit command is:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 run-ontology-audit.py
\end{verbatim}
This invokes the \texttt{OntologyAuditor} class, which performs TTL load validation,
namespace conflict detection, and SPARQL smoke queries against the unified rdflib graph.
The Phase~7 report (\path{open-ontologies-roundtrip-report.md}) was produced by this
command.

The ggen manufacturing pipeline is invoked via:
\begin{verbatim}
ggen generate --config ggen/ggen.toml
\end{verbatim}
This executes the three SPARQL-to-Tera generation rules declared in the manifest,
writing receipted artifacts to \path{sources/wasm4pm-compat/manufactured/} and recording
the run in \path{ggen/.ggen/receipts/}.

Validation experiments are available at:
\begin{verbatim}
python3 adversarial/validate_standards.py
python3 adversarial/simulate_laundering_spoofing.py
python3 experiments/validate_ocpq_refinement.py
python3 experiments/validate_log_fitness.py
\end{verbatim}

\subsection{Templates and Rendered Artifacts}
\label{sec:research-open-ontologies-templates}

Three Tera templates are declared in \path{ggen/ggen.toml}:

\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{templates/witness-marker.tera}]
        Consumes \path{queries/select-witness-projections.rq}. Manufactures
        \path{sources/wasm4pm-compat/manufactured/witnesses/witness-markers-20260601.rs}
        --- a Rust enum encoding the witness lattice for use in wasm4pm-compat's
        type system. Audience: wasm4pm-compat type system. Compliance: lattice
        monotonicity (join-semilattice properties).

  \item[\texttt{templates/process-form.tera}]
        Consumes \path{queries/select-process-forms.rq}. Manufactures
        \path{sources/wasm4pm-compat/manufactured/forms/process-forms-20260601.md}
        --- Markdown documentation of type-law definitions for process forms.
        Audience: Process Intelligence Research Foundry. Compliance: process form
        admissibility boundaries.

  \item[\texttt{templates/wasm-boundary.tera}]
        Consumes \path{queries/select-boundary-crossings.rq}. Manufactures
        \path{sources/wasm4pm-compat/manufactured/boundaries/boundary-law-20260601.wit}
        --- a WebAssembly Interface Types (WIT) boundary definition file.
        Audience: wasm4pm execution engine. Compliance: WASM component model type safety.
\end{description}

All three artifacts are written in \texttt{Overwrite} mode and checksummed with BLAKE3,
with the receipt written to \path{ggen/.ggen/receipts/}.
