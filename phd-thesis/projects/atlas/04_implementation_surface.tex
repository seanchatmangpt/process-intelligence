\section{Implementation Surface}
\label{sec:atlas-impl}

\subsection{Source Files}
\label{sec:atlas-impl-source}

The atlas implementation surface consists exclusively of the seven doctrine
markdown files located at \texttt{/Users/sac/process-intelligence/atlas/}. Each
file was authored on 2026-06-01. No build scripts, Makefiles, Cargo manifests,
or executables reside in the atlas directory. The files and their definitional
scope are:

\begin{description}
  \item[\texttt{aalst\_livestream\_standards.md}] Defines the Aalst Broadcaster
        (Git history $\to$ OCEL stream transformation) and the Conformance Stream
        (ZKP validation diagnostics, WF-net structural alignment, real-time
        severity-rated deviation alerts). Encodes the doctrine: ``If code says it
        worked but the event log cannot prove a lawful process happened, then it
        did not work.''

  \item[\texttt{agi\_adversarial\_defenses.md}] Names the four Iron Law
        safeguards: Schr\"odinger's Interface Fallback, Turing Trap Circuit
        Breakers, Causal Window P2P Constraints, and Hardware Attestation (ZKP).
        This file is intentionally minimal (four bullet points); its expansion
        is an open manufacturing task.

  \item[\texttt{doctrine\_blue\_river\_dam.md}] Defines the Blue River Dam
        doctrine (v30.1.1) and the Mathematical Transition:
        Future Claim (Intent) $\to$ Public Law (Evaluation) $\to$ Actuation
        Boundary (Execution) $\to$ Receipt (Proof). The dam converts
        probabilistic AI output into deterministic power through receipted
        operational manufacturing.

  \item[\texttt{ggen\_prompt\_manufactory.md}] Defines the GGEN transformation
        chain: TTL (Ontology) $\to$ RQ (SPARQL) $\to$ TERA (Templating) $\to$
        Prompt (Warrant). The chain is the generative engine of the manufacturing
        pipeline, transitioning human prose into manufactured research warrants.

  \item[\texttt{supabase\_law\_substrate.md}] Defines the Supabase Law Substrate
        (\texttt{<urn:ostar:law:SupabaseRLS>}), the RLS Authorization Court, and
        the audited RLS Policy Ledger. Tables governed: \texttt{profiles} (with
        \texttt{auth.uid() = id} policies), plus five demonstration-mode tables
        (\texttt{actor\_commands}, \texttt{actor\_events},
        \texttt{actor\_receipts}, \texttt{actor\_outbox},
        \texttt{actor\_quarantine}) flagged for secondary audit before production
        hardening.

  \item[\texttt{wasm\_boundary\_law.md}] Defines the strict separation between
        Specta (Type Surface, TypeScript control) and tsify/wasm-bindgen (Binary
        Surface, WASM ABI). States the law: separation is required to maintain
        stability and receipt integrity across the WASM membrane.

  \item[\texttt{zoeapp\_ministry\_ontology.md}] Encodes Connect, Give, Watch,
        Serve, and Pray as process-laws, and models livestream health as a
        three-state machine (healthy, degraded, critical) with a formal recovery
        path. This file is thin; the formal Petri net or typestate encoding of
        the state machine is an open manufacturing task.
\end{description}

\subsection{Commands}
\label{sec:atlas-impl-commands}

There are no commands internal to the atlas directory. The atlas is a
read-only doctrine surface; it does not expose a CLI, a build target, or an
executable. Downstream commands that consume the atlas are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{GGEN manufacturing pipeline} (from \texttt{/Users/sac/process-intelligence/ggen/}):
        reads TTL ontology files (\texttt{wasm4pm-compat.ttl},
        \texttt{ontology-extensions.ttl}) and manufactures research warrants
        according to the GGEN transformation chain defined in the atlas.
  \item \textbf{Audit scripts} referenced in \texttt{aalst\_livestream\_standards.md}:
        \texttt{run\_audit\_*.sh} scripts check structural integrity and
        alignment; they are external to the atlas directory.
  \item \textbf{BLAKE3 receipt emission protocol}: authorized by
        PROCESS\_INTELLIGENCE\_ALIVE\_001 and referenced in the atlas as the
        cryptographic basis for receipt chains.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Templates and Rendered Artifacts}
\label{sec:atlas-impl-templates}

The atlas does not directly maintain templates. The GGEN manufacturing
infrastructure (\texttt{ggen/rules/} and \texttt{ggen/templates/}) contains
eight rendering templates (Specta TypeScript projection, WASM ABI, WIT schema,
and others) that are governed by the WASM Boundary Law and GGEN Prompt
Manufactory doctrine modules defined in the atlas. These templates are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item TypeScript projection law template (Specta-based)
  \item WASM ABI conversion layer template (tsify/wasm-bindgen-based)
  \item Component Model WIT schema template
  \item Graduation law template
  \item Four additional boundary-specific templates sealed under
        GGEN\_ECOSYSTEM\_INTEL\_ALIVE\_001
\end{itemize}

The atlas names the doctrine that governs these templates (WASM Boundary Law,
GGEN Prompt Manufactory); the templates themselves are manufactured artifacts
that instantiate the named doctrine. The relationship is unidirectional: the
atlas names the law; the templates instantiate it.
