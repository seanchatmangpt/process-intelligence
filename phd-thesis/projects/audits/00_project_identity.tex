\section{Project Identity: Audits Corpus}
\label{sec:audits-identity}

\subsection{Purpose}
\label{sec:audits-identity-purpose}

The audits corpus is the Van der Aalst conformance auditing program for the
process-intelligence research foundry. Its operative doctrine is stated in
\texttt{README\_AUDITS.md}: \emph{``If code says it worked but the event log
cannot prove a lawful process happened, then it did not work.''}

This principle, drawn directly from Van der Aalst's process-mining canon,
governs the corpus's entire structure. The audits directory does not accumulate
reports as documentation artifacts; it accumulates \emph{deterministic,
re-executable receipts} that must be replayed verbatim to confirm the
projection boundaries of the \texttt{wasm4pm-compat} crate remain sound. Every
audit is a reproducible shell script whose output is committed as a receipt.
Re-running any audit must produce byte-identical output, making the corpus a
living executable contract rather than a static archive.

The corpus serves two downstream roles. First, it is the graduation gate for
the \texttt{wasm4pm-compat} projection layer: no checkpoint labeled
\texttt{PROCESS\_INTELLIGENCE\_COMPAT\_CONFORMANCE\_001} may be declared while
any deterministic audit returns \textsc{fail}. Second, it supplies the
board-claim traceability chain: every M\&A claim type must resolve through an
audit-verified receipt before it can be submitted as board-admissible evidence.

\subsection{Repository Surface}
\label{sec:audits-identity-surface}

The audits directory contains 35 files as of 2026-06-01. The surface divides
into five layers.

\textbf{Deterministic shell audits} (\texttt{run\_audit\_1.sh} through
\texttt{run\_audit\_5.sh}) are the executable core. Each script encodes a
single process law as a pattern-matching or structural traversal program and
emits a \textsc{pass} or \textsc{fail} receipt to a paired \texttt{.txt} file.

\textbf{Receipt files} (\texttt{audit-no-dto-flattening.txt},
\texttt{audit-no-tool-smuggling.txt}, \texttt{audit-feature-isolation.txt},
\texttt{audit-projection-receipt.txt}, \texttt{audit-graduation-boundary.txt})
record the committed output of each script execution.

\textbf{Machine-readable consolidation} (\texttt{AUDIT\_LOG.yaml}) captures all
violations, remediation steps, \texttt{GraduationReason} mappings, feature
isolation rules, and the Van der Aalst conformance checklist in structured YAML
format, enabling programmatic downstream consumption.

\textbf{Agent audit reports} cover specialized domains: A* alignment
correctness (\texttt{alignment\_referee\_audit.md}), EWMA concept drift
detection (\texttt{drift\_sentry\_audit.md}), Petri net soundness
(\texttt{petri\_net\_soundness\_audit.md}), SHA-256 ledger integrity
(\texttt{ledger\_custodian\_audit.md}), telemetry XES/OCEL compliance
(\texttt{telemetry\_auditor\_audit.md}), and adversarial corpus certification
(\texttt{adversarial\_audit\_v30.1.1.md}).

\textbf{Research-grade audit documents} cover type-law quality
(\texttt{AUDIT\_TYPE\_LAW\_QUALITY.md}), paper coverage
(\texttt{AUDIT\_PAPER\_COVERAGE.md}), fixture quality
(\texttt{AUDIT\_FIXTURE\_QUALITY.md}), lifecycle completeness
(\texttt{audit-lifecycle-completeness.md}), board claim support
(\texttt{audit-board-claim-support.md}), execution boundaries
(\texttt{audit-execution-boundaries.md}), and neuro-symbolic verification
(\texttt{neuro-symbolic-verification.md}).

\subsection{Primary Research Role}
\label{sec:audits-identity-role}

Within the dissertation, the audits corpus occupies the role of
\emph{completeness audit and compliance verification}. It is the layer that
answers the question: given that a manufacturing pipeline has been declared, can
a process-mining audit of the event evidence confirm that the lawful pipeline
was actually the one that executed?

This role is not administrative. The audits corpus is a direct instantiation of
process-mining Chicago TDD~--- the Van der Aalst Constitution applied to the
research foundry's own codebase. The foundry audits itself using the same event-
log derivation, model-vs-log comparison, and conformance-checking methodology
that the foundry is being built to supply to enterprise customers. The corpus
therefore serves as both a quality control layer and a \emph{proof of concept}
that the methodology is self-applicable.

\subsection{Key Artifacts}
\label{sec:audits-identity-artifacts}

The key artifacts, in priority order, are:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Five deterministic shell audits} (\texttt{run\_audit\_1.sh}
    through \texttt{run\_audit\_5.sh}) with their paired receipt files, forming
    the graduation gate for \texttt{wasm4pm-compat}.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{AUDIT\_LOG.yaml}} --- machine-readable consolidated
    results, violation records, and remediation steps.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{CONFORMANCE\_REPORT.md}} and
    \textbf{\texttt{VAN\_DER\_AALST\_AUDIT\_SUMMARY.txt}} --- human-readable
    executive summaries of the 4/5 PASS / 1 FAIL current status.
  \item \textbf{199 compile-fail fixtures} in
    \texttt{sources/wasm4pm-compat/tests/ui/compile\_fail/} --- the type-law
    receipt corpus with 97.7\% valid law-proving error codes.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{adversarial\_audit\_v30.1.1.md}} --- the
    \texttt{ALIVE\_001} certification covering 567 commits across 12 corpus
    directories, signed with BLAKE3 + Ed25519.
  \item \textbf{\texttt{forensic\_audit\_verdict.md}} --- the clean-room
    forensic verdict confirming 84 passing tests with zero stubs, mocks, or
    hardcoded outputs.
\end{enumerate}
