\section{Open Questions and Next Work}
\label{sec:audits-open}

\subsection{Known Gaps}
\label{sec:audits-open-gaps}

Six classes of known gaps have been identified across the audit corpus as of
2026-06-01.

\textbf{DTO\_001: JSON method naming violation (blocking).} Two method names in
\texttt{wasm4pm-compat}~--- \texttt{to\_json\_string()} at
\texttt{compat/src/manufacturing/mod.rs:735} and \texttt{receipt\_json()} at
\texttt{compat/src/manufacturing/traits.rs:34}~--- violate the DTO flattening
prohibition. The remediation is precisely specified: rename
\texttt{to\_json\_string()} to \texttt{serialize\_receipt()} and
\texttt{receipt\_json()} to \texttt{encode\_receipt\_proof()}. The remediation is
documented but not executed as of this writing.

\textbf{Cross-witness fixture coverage: 38+ pairs untested.} The type-law quality
audit identifies 10+ named witness markers in the witness lattice, yielding
approximately 45 possible pairwise confusion scenarios. Only 7 are currently
covered by explicit compile-fail fixtures. The 7 covered pairs address the most
important combinations (OCEL vs.\ XES, XES vs.\ WF-net, OCEL vs.\ POWL), but 38
or more pairwise non-forgeability receipts remain unmanufactured. This leaves the
non-forgeability claim of the \texttt{Evidence<T>} type partially unreceipted.

\textbf{Ontology rebranding: ZOEapp to PCP.} The \texttt{rebranding\_ontology\_audit.md}
identifies required renames across 4 or more TTL ontology files from ZOEapp
namespace to PCP namespace. The audit lists findings but does not confirm the
renames were applied. The current rebranding status is unresolved.

\textbf{Lifecycle completeness: 5 residual gaps.} The lifecycle completeness
audit (\texttt{audit-lifecycle-completeness.md}) identifies five residual gaps
where wasm4pm execution boundary conditions are not mapped to corresponding
lifecycle stage specifications: RESIDUAL-DESIGN-001 (wasm4pm execution boundary
conditions not mapped to Design-stage model constraints), RESIDUAL-SIM-001
(memory and fuel constraints not mapped to simulation-time complexity estimation),
RESIDUAL-SIM-002 (no receipt format specification for simulation proof
reachability witnesses), RESIDUAL-MON-001, and RESIDUAL-MON-002 (Monitoring
stage wasm4pm authority gaps).

\textbf{Telemetry compliance conflict: XES/OCEL verdict discrepancy.} The
telemetry auditor (Agent~2) found a \textsc{fail} on XES/OCEL compliance~--- 0\%
OCEL 2.0 compliance and a 2/5 XES score. The stream director (Agent~1) declared
\textsc{pass} after DOM remediation on the same codebase surface. These two
verdicts are in partial conflict and have not been reconciled. The conflicting
verdicts constitute an open audit finding that must be resolved before the
telemetry surface can be declared conformant.

\textbf{Graduation boundary sealing: thin fixture coverage.} The type-law quality
audit notes that the graduation boundary sealing is documented primarily in
docstrings and \texttt{CLAUDE.md} rather than type-sealed by compile-fail
fixtures. Only two fixtures explicitly test engine boundary rejection
(\texttt{conformance\_checker\_absent.rs} and
\texttt{dfg\_engine\_boundary\_rejected.rs}). At minimum, one engine-boundary
compile-fail fixture per \texttt{GraduationReason} variant is recommended.

\subsection{Research Risks}
\label{sec:audits-open-risks}

Three research risks are assessed as non-trivial.

\textbf{Verdict drift between agent audits.} The telemetry auditor / stream
director conflict demonstrates that agent audits targeting the same surface can
reach contradictory verdicts. As the audit corpus grows, the risk of contradictory
verdicts increases. Without a defined precedence rule (e.g., a more recent audit
supersedes an older one for the same surface), contradictions accumulate as
unresolved debt. [AUTHOR THESIS: a formal audit precedence protocol is required.]

\textbf{Fixture corpus drift.} The compile-fail fixture corpus proves law at the
type-system level, but only for the type-law surface that was declared when the
fixture was written. As the \texttt{wasm4pm-compat} crate evolves, fixtures may
become stale~--- testing a type that no longer exists, or missing a type that was
added. The zero-\texttt{E0425} finding confirms the corpus is clean today, but
maintaining this property requires a fixture maintenance protocol.

\textbf{Ontology-code surface misalignment.} The rebranding audit finding~---
where TTL ontology files still carry ZOEapp namespace labels while the code has
moved to PCP branding~--- illustrates a systematic risk: ontology surfaces evolve
on a different cadence than code surfaces, and without a continuous audit
coupling them, they drift. This is the process-intelligence equivalent of the
enterprise process gap: the declared ontology is not the executed ontology.
