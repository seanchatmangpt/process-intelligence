\section{Contribution to the Dissertation}
\label{sec:sources-wasm4pm-compat-contribution}

\subsection{Contribution to Prediction vs.~Coordination}
\label{sec:sources-wasm4pm-compat-contribution-prediction}

The dissertation's central claim is that process intelligence is fundamentally about
coordination---producing receipted, non-forgeable evidence that a lawful process
occurred---rather than prediction of what a process is likely to do. The
\texttt{wasm4pm-compat} type-law foundry provides the formal infrastructure that
makes this distinction operational.

A prediction system produces a probability distribution over future states. A coordination
system produces an \texttt{Evidence<T, State, Witness>} block: a cryptographically bound,
lattice-monotonic, authority-signed record that a specific state transition was observed
in a specific process execution with a specific witness. These are not interchangeable.
A fitness score of 0.94 emitted without a BLAKE3 hash binding it to its trace, its model,
and its alignment witness is a prediction about process quality. The same fitness score
emitted inside an \texttt{Evidence} block with an \texttt{ArtifactReceipt} at witness
level \texttt{Adriansyah2011} is a coordination fact.

The type-law foundry makes the structural distinction between these two kinds of claims
inexpressible in the opposite direction: the Rust type system prevents a coordination
fact from being minted without the required cryptographic and lattice structure. This is
the type-foundry's primary contribution to the prediction-vs-coordination argument.

\subsection{Contribution to Process Evidence}
\label{sec:sources-wasm4pm-compat-contribution-evidence}

The \texttt{wasm4pm-compat} project contributes the formal definition of what constitutes
process evidence at the type level. Prior to this work, process evidence in the research
program was described in prose: event logs, conformance scores, alignment costs. The
type-law foundry operationalizes that prose as a Rust type system enforced at compile time.

The specific contribution is the five-domain type-law surface documented in
\texttt{type-law-atlas.md}: (1) the \texttt{Evidence<T, State, Witness>} type law
with three admissibility axioms, (2) the witness lattice $(W, \sqsubseteq, \sqcup,
\bot, \top)$ with idempotency, commutativity, associativity, and absorption properties,
(3) the admission/refusal law with default-deny semantics and four refusal classes,
(4) the loss policy thermodynamics with permissible and absolute loss boundaries,
and (5) the graduation boundary with seven trigger signals. Together these five domains
constitute a complete formal specification of what process evidence is, what it may
contain, and when it must be rejected.

The \texttt{WitnessMarker} lattice also contributes a citation map: each marker
names a specific publication (Van der Aalst 1989, Murata 1989, Van der Aalst 1998,
Van der Aalst 2016, Weijters 2011, Leemans 2013, Adriansyah 2011) as the authority
for its algebraic proof structure, grounding the type system in the published literature.

\subsection{Contribution to Manufacturing Architecture}
\label{sec:sources-wasm4pm-compat-contribution-manufacturing}

The project demonstrates that a type-law system can itself be manufactured from an
ontological source of truth. The \texttt{ggen/wasm4pm-compat.ttl} RDF ontology is the
authoritative declaration of the type system; the three SPARQL queries and three Tera
templates are the manufacturing pipeline; the three sealed artifacts are the manufactured
artifacts. This is not code generation in the conventional sense---it is ontology-driven
law manufacturing, where the ontology encodes the type laws and the templates materialize
them as Rust, Markdown, and WIT.

This architecture establishes a pattern that other projects in the research program can
follow: declare the law in RDF, manufacture the enforcement surface via SPARQL and Tera,
receipt the artifacts via the \texttt{ReceiptLedger}, and verify the manufacturing via
the compliance auditor. The pattern is receipted end-to-end in the
\texttt{manufacturing-manifest-20260601.yaml}, which records the ontology source, query
results, template rendering statistics, compliance audit results, and receipt ledger
entries in a single authority document.

\subsection{Contribution to Capital-Flow Infrastructure}
\label{sec:sources-wasm4pm-compat-contribution-capital}

The capital-flow and M\&A tier of the dissertation requires board-admissible evidence:
claims about process quality that can withstand due diligence scrutiny because they are
cryptographically bound to the event logs and process models that produced them.
\texttt{wasm4pm-compat} contributes the type-level infrastructure that makes
board-admissible evidence manufacturable.

The specific mechanism is the receipt-shaped object: an \texttt{Evidence} block where
\texttt{State} is terminal (the process has completed), \texttt{Witness} carries fitness
$\geq$ threshold (the conformance measurement is above the admissibility floor), and the
hash is signed by an auditor role (the signing authority has been verified). This
receipt-shaped object is the primitive from which M\&A claims are built. The
\texttt{GraduationCandidate<T, W>} struct carries the reviewed case that bridges from
the type-law layer to the M\&A claim layer: a named \texttt{GraduationReason}, a
\texttt{subject} identifying the process under review, an \texttt{evidence\_ref} pointing
to the receipt, and the preserved witness.

The downstream obligation to align receipt-shaped object schemas with the M\&A slide-to-receipt
map (recorded in \texttt{research-verdict.md} Section~2.3 as a gap) confirms that the
connection to capital-flow infrastructure is architecturally intended and structurally
prepared, even if the final alignment is a future work item.
