## 5. Force 3: Buyer Integration & Lock-In

### 5.1 Traditional Model (Customer Power)

Porter: Buyers have power when:
- Few sellers exist
- Products are interchangeable
- Switching costs are low

Traditional response: Differentiate, invest in customer relationships, raise switching costs.

### 5.2 Reverse Model (Buyers as Topology Nodes)

**Buyers are no longer customers; they are integrated execution nodes in our process.**

**Strategy:** Model the buyer's operational states as a subset of the global topology.

**Mathematical Formulation:**

Let $B$ be a buyer (e.g., a customer enterprise).

We model the buyer's operational states as:
$$P_{\text{buyer}} \subset P_{\text{std}}$$

The buyer's actions update the global marking:
$$M_{\text{std}} \xrightarrow{t_{\text{buyer}}} M'_{\text{std}}$$

This means:
1. **Every buyer action is a state transition in our net** — Their purchase orders, shipment confirmations, payment transfers are transitions in $W_{\text{std}}$
2. **Their data is bound into our ledger** — All buyer actions generate unforgeable BLAKE3 receipts
3. **Their operations depend on our model** — Any deviation from our process topology causes their transactions to fail
4. **They cannot unilaterally change the model** — Altering the buyer's state representation requires consensus on $W_{\text{std}}$ updates

### 5.3 Buyer Integration Mechanisms

**Mechanism 1: Operational Dependency**
- Buyer's purchase orders are TOs in $W_{\text{std}}$
- Fulfillment requires conformance to our defined process model
- Any deviation → transaction rejection

**Mechanism 2: Data Lock-In (Paradoxically via Open Standards)**
- Buyer's operational history is encoded in standard formats (OCEL, XES)
- Data is not locked in proprietary format
- BUT switching requires finding a vendor operating under the same $W_{\text{std}}$ model
- All compliant vendors execute the same process constraints

**Mechanism 3: Economic Switching Costs**
- Buyer adapts their internal workflows to our model
- Buyer's suppliers conform to our model
- Buyer's customers interact via our ledger
- To exit: Buyer must:
  - Rebuild all supplier relationships with non-conformant providers
  - Rebuild all customer connections with non-conformant consumers
  - Operate outside the verified economy (losing credibility, auditability, proof)

**Result:** Switching is technically free but economically impossible.

### 5.4 Buyer Lock-In Benefits

By integrating buyers into our topology:

- Customer growth drives our topology expansion
- Customer cannot exit without breaking all downstream relationships
- Customer economic stickiness → **infinite** (embedded in their operational graph)

## 6. Force 4: Hyper-Substitution Absorption

### 6.1 Traditional Model (Substitution Threat)

Porter: Substitutes threaten when:
- Alternative products perform the same function
- Switching to substitutes is easy
- Price/performance of substitutes is favorable

Traditional response: Differentiate, raise switching costs, invest in brand loyalty.

### 6.2 Reverse Model (Converting Substitutes into Noise)

**We want substitutes. We mathematically project them into irrelevance.**

**Strategy:** Define a projection function that maps all competitive actions to standardized labels in our model, rendering competitor features as invisible transitions ($\tau$).

**Mathematical Formulation:**

Let $\Sigma_{\text{comp}}$ be the activity alphabet of a competing system.
Let $\Sigma_{\text{std}}$ be the activity alphabet of our standard model.

We define a projection function:
$$f_{\text{sub}} : \Sigma_{\text{comp}} \to \Sigma_{\text{std}} \cup \{\tau\}$$

Where:
- $f_{\text{sub}}(e) = \ell \quad \text{if } \ell = e \text{ and } e \in \Sigma_{\text{std}}$ (standard activity)
- $f_{\text{sub}}(e) = \tau \quad \text{if } e \notin \Sigma_{\text{std}}$ (competitor's unique feature becomes invisible)

**Example Projection:**

Competitor's activities:
- "Create Order" → maps to our "Place Order" ✓
- "Validate Inventory" → invisible (hidden in our single "Check Stock" step)
- "Apply Discount Tier V3" → invisible (competitor's proprietary logic collapses to τ)
- "Async Retry Loop" → invisible (we execute synchronously)

**Result:** The competitor's differentiating features are **mathematically projected out**. From the ledger perspective, they execute identically to any other compliant vendor.

### 6.3 Substitution Absorption Benefits

By projecting competitor features into invisibility:
- Competitors cannot differentiate via proprietary logic (we make it irrelevant)
- Competitor features are unauditable (they map to τ, which leaves no trace in the verified economy)
- Competitor advantage = unverifiable claim
